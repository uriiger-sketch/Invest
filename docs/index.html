Methodology

Every crawl gathers, per company: analyst consensus and its 1–3-month trend, price targets (mean, median, dispersion), each firm's rating and price-target changes, EPS estimate revisions and their breadth, the latest earnings surprise and next report date, valuation, profitability and short interest, headlines from Yahoo Finance and Google News (scored with a finance-specific lexicon), SEC filings (8-K red-flag items, late-filing notices, 13D activist stakes, offerings), Form 4 insider trades and 13F institutional positions.

Each signal is rank-normalised to N(0,1) across the universe (partially sector-neutralised for valuation, profitability, short interest and target upside). Each carries a literature prior for its information coefficient (IC) per horizon, updated by the IC measured on this system's own history (precision-weighted Bayes). Weights are Σ⁻¹·IC, so correlated signals share weight instead of double-counting. A LightGBM ranker joins only in proportion to its purged out-of-sample IC. The grade combines the four horizon scores weighted by their estimated skill and correlation; α = IC·σ·z (Grinold) and P(beat) = Φ(IC·z) follow from the same model. Missing data counts as neutral, never as a fabricated value; the confidence column shows how much of the model each grade actually rests on.

Israel board: Tel Aviv lines rarely have the analyst coverage the picks table requires, so every Israeli company with clean prices and at least $0.5M daily traded value (converted from agorot at the day's exchange rate) is scored by the same model and placed on the picks table's distribution: all standardisation, blend weights and grade percentiles come from the gated names, which the board therefore cannot move.

Honest scale: realistic ICs are 0.02–0.08, so even an A+ carries a probability of beating the universe of only slightly above 50 % per month. This is a research shortlist, not investment advice.
